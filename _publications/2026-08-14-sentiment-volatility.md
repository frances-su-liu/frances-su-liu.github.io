## Abstract

Does media sentiment improve realized volatility forecasts, and if so, when? We construct media-based predictors in four families (direction, salience, instability and cross-channel disagreement) from firm-level news and social-media sentiment and attention indices, compress them by principal components, and add them to HAR models of daily realized volatility for DJIA constituents over nineteen years. In sample, the predictors raise conditional-quantile fit in the upper tail, most for the least persistent stocks. Out of sample, no augmented model robustly beats a daily re-estimated HAR benchmark in any state identifiable at the forecast origin. A regime-conditional evaluation that sorts forecast errors by the realized volatility of the target day shows gains where high volatility meets low persistence; we show that selecting on the outcome produces this pattern by rewarding any forecast shifted toward the realized surprise. A pre-specified forecast combination recovers a small gain in one real-time state.

**Keywords:** Media sentiment; Volatility forecasting; Realized volatility; Volatility persistence; State-dependent predictability; HAR model; Forecast evaluation.
